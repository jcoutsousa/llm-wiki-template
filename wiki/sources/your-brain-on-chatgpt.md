# Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt

## Executive Summary

This MIT Media Lab study investigates the cognitive cost of using Large Language Models (LLMs) for essay writing tasks. Using EEG to measure brain activity, the research found that LLM-assisted writing produces measurable decreases in neural connectivity and cognitive engagement compared to brain-only and search engine approaches.

## Key Findings

- **Neural Connectivity**: Brain-only group showed strongest, widest-ranging neural networks; Search Engine group intermediate; LLM group weakest overall coupling
- **Cognitive Debt**: LLM group showed reduced neural connectivity and under-engagement of alpha and beta networks, indicating cognitive offloading
- **Session 4 Results**: LLM-to-Brain (participants who used LLM first, then brain-only) showed weaker neural connectivity; Brain-to-LLM showed higher memory recall and re-engagement of widespread occipito-parietal and prefrontal nodes
- **Ownership**: LLM group reported low ownership of their essays; Search Engine group had strong ownership but less than Brain-only group
- **Quoting Ability**: LLM group fell behind in ability to quote from their own essays

## Methodology

- 54 participants across three groups: LLM, Search Engine, Brain-only
- 3 sessions with same group assignment; 4th session crossed over groups
- EEG recording during essay writing tasks using dDTF analysis
- NLP analysis of essays
- Human teacher and AI judge scoring

## Impact

Demonstrates measurable cognitive impacts of LLM use in educational contexts. While initial benefits appear apparent, LLM users performed worse than counterparts at neural, linguistic, and scoring levels over the 4-month study period.
